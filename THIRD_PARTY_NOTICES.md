# Third-party notices

miniDaw's own code is MIT-licensed (see `package.json`). The recorded instrument
samples in `src/assets/samples/` come from the open sample libraries below; the drum
grooves the arrangement coach plays come from the Groove MIDI Dataset (see the end). Each sample
set was **modified** for the web: selected notes / velocity layers / round robins, microphones mixed
to stereo (drum kits), resampled to 44.1 kHz, silence before the attack trimmed, long decays
shortened with a fade-out, sustain loops unrolled (Rhodes, steel guitar), each set normalised
as a whole, and encoded as MP3. Each note also carries a playback gain (stored in
`src/audio/samples/sets.json`) that evens out its loudness against its neighbours, so a melody
does not jump in level from note to note. The scripts that do this are in `scripts/samples/`.

The app also shows these credits under 「檔案 → 音色來源與授權」.

## Drum performances — Groove MIDI Dataset (CC BY 4.0)

The arrangement coach's drum grooves and fills for pop, Mandopop, ballad, rock, country,
boom bap, lo-fi, R&B, funk, city pop (including its disco choruses) and jazz
(`src/music/grooves/gmd.json`) are excerpted from the **Groove MIDI Dataset v1.0.0** by
Jon Gillick, Adam Roberts, Jesse Engel, Douglas Eck and David Bamman (Google Magenta),
licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) —
https://magenta.tensorflow.org/datasets/groove

> Jon Gillick, Adam Roberts, Jesse Engel, Douglas Eck, and David Bamman.
> "Learning to Groove with Inverse Sequence Transformations."
> International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2019.

**Changes:** two-bar grooves and one-bar fills excerpted from the performances; timing
converted to beats and centred on the click (the drummer's push and drag kept); velocities
scaled per drum piece; occasional rimshot / cross-stick zone triggers made consistent; the
Roland TD-11 kit mapped to General MIDI pieces. The script that does this is
`scripts/grooves/build_grooves.py`.

## Arranging references

The melody generator (`src/music/generate/melody.ts`) follows the phrase-structure,
embellishing-tone and pop-form principles taught in **Open Music Theory** by Kris P. Shaffer,
Bryn Hughes and Brian Moseley (Hybrid Pedagogy Publishing,
[CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)) —
https://openmusictheory.github.io. No text or examples from the book are included.

The coach's notes on how an arrangement breathes (`src/music/breathing.ts`, used by
`src/music/generate/arrange.ts`) and its three classic songs per genre
(`src/music/genres/classics.json`) summarise, in our own words, public songwriting and production
articles (Open Music Theory, Secrets of Songwriting, Ableton's *Making Music*, Soundfly,
Sound On Sound, Musical U, Wikipedia and others). The app links every source; no text, audio or
transcriptions of the songs are included.
